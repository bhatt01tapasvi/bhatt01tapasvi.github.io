Smart 3-in-1 Plastic Separation System

At the University of Tokyo research camp (July 2025), I worked in a 46-member global cohort from 10 universities. Mission: build a practical, high-throughput plastic sorting system for real recycling lines.

  • Event: University of Tokyo International Summer Research Camp
  • Timeline: July 2025
  • Team: Nadia Oktiarsy (UTokyo), Takumi Endo (UTokyo), Ting-Wei Emily Chang (Rice University), Zheng Zhou (ZJU), and Yours Truly ☺ (NTU)
  • Award: Best Innovation

Our architecture combines electrostatic separation, acoustic resonance, and electromagnetic control. It hit 90-95% sorting accuracy on mixed plastic streams and stayed stable even with messy input feed.

How it works:

1. Electrostatic Separation: Plastics get triboelectrically charged as they slide along the belt, then pass through a high-voltage electric field (~40 kV) where charge differences cause lateral deflection, achieving >95% sorting accuracy.

2. Acoustic Resonance Separation: Using ~40 Hz acoustic waves, plastics vibrate differently based on their stiffness (Young's modulus), allowing separation with ~90% accuracy through resonance amplitude variations.

3. Electromagnetic (EM) Separation: The breakthrough component. Pulsed EM fields at 60 kHz and 100 kHz target differential dielectric polarization dynamics. PVC, with chlorine atoms in its polymer chain, has higher dielectric loss and sticks to the belt longer at 100 kHz, while PET slides off, enabling >95% separation accuracy.

My primary contribution focused on the physics and system modeling for the electromagnetic module, tuning the pulsed field frequencies and stick-slip parameters to cleanly separate PET and PVC. I coordinated with external APAC collaborators to validate our models and developed comprehensive testing plans for handling dielectric polarization with ionic dynamics on a conveyor belt.

Impact and Specifications: Our prototype processes 50 to 1,500 kg/hour depending on setup, with estimated cost around US$118k to 195k including maintenance. As global plastic use grows, this system can reduce contamination, improve sorting quality, and keep industrial throughput practical.

After weeks of equations, design reviews, and rapid prototyping, we shipped a working system with strong reliability and practical complexity. Not just a demo. It is a serious step toward circular manufacturing.

NTU Team Experience: Special thanks to Prof. Yang Jianfei, who arranged this incredible opportunity for us NTU students and captured many memorable moments throughout the camp.

This project won Best Innovation and gave us a clean blueprint for scaling smarter sorting systems.

Particle Image Velocimetry

I use PIV pipelines with Vicsek-model thinking and cross-correlation tracking to study collective motion. Focus areas are flow field reconstruction, velocity coherence, and emergent dynamics in biological swarms.

Heat Transfer Enhancement

This work targets high-performance cooling in mini-channel systems for solar, EV thermal management, and industrial exchangers. The toolbox includes magnetic nanofluids, vibration control, and external magnetic forcing.

We study how vibration frequency and magnetic field strength reshape boundary layers and secondary flows. Target is higher heat transfer with controlled pressure penalty. Results are published in Results in Engineering and Numerical Heat Transfer.

We also run exergy analysis to track energy quality, not only temperature reduction. End goal is robust thermal design that survives real operating constraints.

  • Timeline: January 2022 - Present
  • Mentors: Dr. Suvanjan Bhattacharyya
  • Collaborators: Naman Jain, Ali Cemal Benim

CRYOMOVE: Predicting Cold Chain Failure

PCM, or phase change material, is what keeps a vaccine box cold without power. The problem is that it does not melt at a fixed rate. It depends on the outside temperature, which keeps changing during a delivery. Re-running a full CFD simulation every time the temperature shifts is too slow to be useful in the field.

So instead, we trained a small neural network on simulation data to learn the melt curve directly. Sensors on the box send live temperature readings to the cloud every 10 minutes, the model predicts how much longer the PCM has before it melts, and cold chain managers get a warning before the box actually fails, not after. It is built to extend beyond vaccines too, into food and general cold storage.

  • Methods: ANSYS Fluent, artificial neural networks, IoT telemetry, cloud inference
  • Collaborators: Manit Baser, Abhishek Tyagi, Prof. Eddie Yin Kwee Ng

PCM Formulation

One direct way to keep a cold box cold for longer is to slow down the ice itself. We added a small amount of glycerol, about 5%, to n-tetradecane, a common cold chain PCM, and ran numerical simulations to see what changed.

The additive stretched the safe cooling window from about 123 hours to about 144 hours, roughly 20 extra hours, and the mixture stayed thermally stable for longer too. It is a simple, cheap tweak to the material itself, no new hardware needed, with a real payoff for how long a vaccine stays safe in transit.

  • Methods: Numerical simulation, entropy analysis, n-tetradecane + glycerol PCM
  • Collaborators: Naman Jain, Prof. Eddie Yin Kwee Ng

Passive Insulation

PCM only works well if the box around it is not leaking heat. We tested epoxy coatings and multilayer aluminium foil insulation on eutectic cold boxes under tropical conditions, 32-34°C and 75-85% humidity, over a full week.

Dual-layer foil cut heat gain by 57%, and the coated boxes held sub-5°C temperatures for 7 days versus the usual 2-3 days that most unpowered boxes manage. All of it with no electricity, which matters most in places where power is not reliable.

  • Methods: Experimental thermal testing, epoxy coatings, multilayer foil insulation
  • Collaborators: Hamid Saeedipour, Nikhil Sachdeva, Antara Chakraborty, Chew Tong Howe, Prof. Eddie Yin Kwee Ng

Fast Simulation

Full 3D CFD gets the physics right but is too slow for quick design iteration. We built a reduced-order model using the Lattice Boltzmann Method that runs a fast 2D GPU simulation and adds a physics-informed correction for the 3D heat paths it misses.

The correction is calibrated once on a real box and then reused across different payloads in the same enclosure, no re-tuning needed. Tested against real cold-box experiments plus an independent dataset, it matched real temperatures closely while running far cheaper than full CFD, making it practical for fast design iteration.

  • Methods: Lattice Boltzmann Method, GPU simulation, physics-informed correction
  • Collaborators: Soham Jain, Prof. Eddie Yin Kwee Ng

Bio-Based Materials

Most cold chain insulation is foam: energy-intensive to make, and it does not biodegrade. We grew mycelium from Ganoderma lucidum on waste cardboard, rice husk, and oats, and let the fungus bind the waste into a bio-fiber envelope.

It insulates about as well as foam, is hydrophobic, fire-resistant, and fully biodegradable, with far lower carbon emissions and energy demand than typical cold chain materials. We paired it with a digital twin for real-time monitoring, since a bio-material's performance can vary more than a manufactured foam's. Still early-stage: results hold for the prototype sizes and conditions tested so far, and scaling it up is ongoing work.

  • Methods: Mycelium biofabrication, digital twin monitoring, materials characterization
  • Collaborators: Deepak Sharma, Prof. Eddie Yin Kwee Ng, Prof. Hortense Le Ferrand
