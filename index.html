Research

Keeping temperature-sensitive goods cold without power: materials, simulation, and failure prediction.

Cold-chain thermal systems

Vaccines and other temperature-sensitive goods have to stay within a narrow range from factory to clinic, often without reliable power along the way. My work covers the full chain: the phase change material that holds the temperature, the insulation around it, and a neural network (CRYOMOVE) trained on ANSYS Fluent simulation data that reads live sensor data and predicts a temperature failure before it happens, not after.

  • Passive insulation holds sub-5°C for 7 days versus the usual 2-3, with no power input
  • Dual-layer foil insulation cuts heat gain by 57%
  • A 5% glycerol additive extends the safe cooling window by roughly 20 hours

PCM Formulation

Adding 5% glycerol to n-tetradecane, a common cold-chain PCM, stretches the safe cooling window from about 123 to about 144 hours, a simple material change with no new hardware.

Passive Insulation

Epoxy coatings and multilayer aluminium foil on eutectic cold boxes, tested at 32-34°C and 75-85% humidity. Dual-layer foil cuts heat gain by 57% with no power input.

Fast Simulation

A reduced-order Lattice Boltzmann model that runs a fast 2D GPU simulation with a physics-informed correction for the 3D heat paths full CFD would otherwise be needed for.

Bio-Based Materials

Mycelium grown on waste cardboard, rice husk, and oats into a bio-fiber insulation envelope: hydrophobic, fire-resistant, fully biodegradable, and comparable to foam on insulating performance.

Smart 3-in-1 Plastic Separation System

At the University of Tokyo research camp (July 2025), I worked in a 46-member global cohort from 10 universities. Mission: build a practical, high-throughput plastic sorting system for real recycling lines.

  • Event: University of Tokyo International Summer Research Camp
  • Timeline: July 2025
  • Team: Nadia Oktiarsy (UTokyo), Takumi Endo (UTokyo), Ting-Wei Emily Chang (Rice University), Zheng Zhou (ZJU), and Yours Truly ☺ (NTU)
  • Award: Best Innovation

Our architecture combines electrostatic separation, acoustic resonance, and electromagnetic control. It hit 90-95% sorting accuracy on mixed plastic streams and stayed stable even with messy input feed.

How it works:

1. Electrostatic Separation: Plastics get triboelectrically charged as they slide along the belt, then pass through a high-voltage electric field (~40 kV) where charge differences cause lateral deflection, achieving >95% sorting accuracy.

2. Acoustic Resonance Separation: Using ~40 Hz acoustic waves, plastics vibrate differently based on their stiffness (Young's modulus), allowing separation with ~90% accuracy through resonance amplitude variations.

3. Electromagnetic (EM) Separation: The breakthrough component. Pulsed EM fields at 60 kHz and 100 kHz target differential dielectric polarization dynamics. PVC, with chlorine atoms in its polymer chain, has higher dielectric loss and sticks to the belt longer at 100 kHz, while PET slides off, enabling >95% separation accuracy.

My primary contribution focused on the physics and system modeling for the electromagnetic module, tuning the pulsed field frequencies and stick-slip parameters to cleanly separate PET and PVC. I coordinated with external APAC collaborators to validate our models and developed comprehensive testing plans for handling dielectric polarization with ionic dynamics on a conveyor belt.

Impact and Specifications: Our prototype processes 50 to 1,500 kg/hour depending on setup, with estimated cost around US$118k to 195k including maintenance. As global plastic use grows, this system can reduce contamination, improve sorting quality, and keep industrial throughput practical.

After weeks of equations, design reviews, and rapid prototyping, we shipped a working system with strong reliability and practical complexity. Not just a demo. It is a serious step toward circular manufacturing.

NTU Team Experience: Special thanks to Prof. Yang Jianfei, who arranged this incredible opportunity for us NTU students and captured many memorable moments throughout the camp.

This project won Best Innovation and gave us a clean blueprint for scaling smarter sorting systems.
